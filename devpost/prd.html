The core journey

Click a step in the diagram, or use Back / Next. Source: scope.md > The Core Loop

1 · Arrive (first time)

The student opens StudyFlow AI and sees a welcoming screen explaining that the app builds a realistic plan from their exams, topics and available time.

2 · Set up, step by step

Subjects → exam date and topics per subject (name, estimated time, difficulty, confidence) → daily availability → Generate My Study Plan. Never one long form.

3 · See the plan

The dashboard shows today's sessions with status, plus a simple overview of upcoming exams.

4 · Work the plan

Each session is marked Completed, Skipped or Partially Completed, with optional actual time spent and an updated confidence.

5 · Fall behind

A scheduled day passes unmarked (shown as “Unconfirmed”), or sessions are skipped or only partly finished.

6 · Replan

Click Replan My Schedule. If any sessions are still Unconfirmed, a confirmation step comes first. After a loading state, only the affected sessions are shown, original → revised, with the AI's explanation.

7 · Decide

The student accepts the revised plan or keeps the original.

8 · Success

The revised plan visibly differs in priorities, time allocations and remaining workload, and the AI explains why. The plan responded to real progress.

Setup and availability scope.md > What “Working” Looks Like

The student enters subjects, then per subject an exam date and topics (name, estimated time, difficulty, confidence). Availability is per day of the week: weekday and weekend hours with optional overrides, editable later; the AI uses the updated limits on the next replan.

  • Setup is a step-by-step flow, not one long form.
  • I can add several subjects, each with an exam date and several topics.
  • Each topic captures name, estimated time, difficulty (1–5) and confidence (1–5); values outside 1–5 can't be entered. 1 = easy / not confident, 5 = very hard / very confident
  • I can set weekday and weekend hours and override individual days.
  • Generate My Study Plan produces a plan and opens the dashboard.
Editing after setup new: your decision

From Settings / Edit the student can add a topic, change an exam date, or update difficulty and confidence, without restarting. The next replan uses those edits.

  • I can add a topic, change an exam date, and update a topic's difficulty or confidence without restarting.
  • The edits I make are the ones the next replan uses.
Start Over (Reset Demo)

A simple option that clears all data and returns to the initial setup, useful for demonstrating the app several times during the hackathon.

  • Start Over is reachable from the dashboard.
  • After confirming, all stored data is cleared and the welcome/setup flow appears as on first use.
Plan generation scope.md > The Core Loop

The system generates a daily plan from today until the last exam, using AI for prioritization and planning decisions while the application enforces hard constraints (daily hours, exam dates, topic time). Weak, high-stakes topics come first; days are never overloaded; room is left for breaks.

  • Every session shows subject, topic, planned duration, priority and status.
  • No day's total planned time exceeds that day's available hours.
  • Lower-confidence, higher-difficulty topics near an exam get higher priority than strong topics.
Session tracking

Mark each session Completed, Skipped or Partially Completed; record actual time spent. Confidence can be updated when completing or partially completing, and the AI uses the latest level. For a partial session, the unfinished portion counts as remaining work at the next replan. Progress shows only as completed vs. unfinished sessions.

  • Each session offers Completed / Skipped / Partially Completed.
  • I can enter actual time spent for a session.
  • When I mark a session Completed or Partially Completed, I can update my confidence in that topic.
  • If I mark a 2-hour session partially done with 1 hour spent, the next replan treats about 1 hour as remaining.
  • Status changes show immediately and remain after closing and reopening the app.
Unconfirmed sessions

A past, unmarked session is “Unconfirmed”, not auto-skipped, because the student may have done it offline. Clicking Replan with any open triggers an explicit confirmation step:

  1. A list of the Unconfirmed sessions appears before any AI request.
  2. For each one, mark it Completed / Skipped / Partially Completed right there.
  3. Choose Replan with these updates, Replan anyway (the rest are treated as missed), or Cancel.
  4. “Replan anyway” treats them as missed for this replan only; their status stays “Unconfirmed”. (Small assumption: confirm in review.)
  5. No Unconfirmed sessions? This step is skipped.
  • A past, unmarked session displays “Unconfirmed”, not “Skipped”.
  • Replan with Unconfirmed sessions shows the confirmation step, listing them, before any AI request.
  • In that step I can mark any of them, then replan.
  • “Replan anyway” proceeds with the rest treated as missed; their status stays “Unconfirmed”.
  • “Cancel” returns me to the dashboard unchanged.
  • With none Unconfirmed, Replan starts immediately.
Replan My Schedule the kernel scope.md > The Unique Kernel

The AI reassesses remaining tasks, exams, session outcomes, actual time, difficulty, confidence and current availability. It never blindly pushes everything to tomorrow: it prioritizes, postpones or shortens, and avoids unrealistic workloads.

Who decides what: the AI prioritizes and explains; the application validates the rules before anything is shown: no day over its hours, nothing after its exam or in the past, no topic over its remaining time, every topic with work left either scheduled or listed as Not scheduled, and explanation numbers matching the plan. A proposal that breaks a rule is never shown; the student sees the failure message and can retry.

“I moved Database Architecture revision to Thursday because your Transaction Scheduling topic is less familiar and your exam is approaching.”

  • After clicking, a loading state appears, then only the changed sessions display, each original → revised.
  • Unchanged sessions are not duplicated in the review view.
  • A proposal that breaks an enforced rule is never shown; I see the failure message with Retry.
  • Each moved, shortened or prioritized session is explained in plain language.
  • Accept replaces the active plan; Keep original leaves it unchanged.
  • The revised plan visibly differs in priorities, time allocations or remaining workload (demo: original → missed session → revised).
Not enough time: honest trade-offs the kernel

If everything can't fit before an exam, the AI prioritizes by exam proximity, difficulty and confidence, respects hours and breaks, and lists what couldn't fit as Not scheduled with the reason.

“Your exam is in two days, and you have four study hours available, but your remaining topics require six hours… Database Architecture revision couldn't fit and is listed below as ‘Not scheduled.’”

  • When required time exceeds available time, the explanation states both numbers.
  • Topics that couldn't fit appear in “Not scheduled” with a reason each.
  • Scheduled sessions never exceed the available hours.

States and what-ifs

The edge cases you resolved, and exactly what the student sees.

First use

No plan yet, so the student sees onboarding, not an empty dashboard.

Unconfirmed session

Past and unmarked: shown as “Unconfirmed” until the student resolves it. Replan asks to confirm or proceed.

Replan confirmation

If Unconfirmed sessions exist, a confirmation step comes first: mark them, replan anyway, or cancel.

Replan loading

A visible loading state while the revised plan is generated.

Replan not needed

“You're on track! Your current plan still fits your deadlines and availability.” No changes are made just to look useful.

Replan failed, too slow, or the proposal broke a rule

Original plan unchanged. “We couldn't generate your revised plan. Your current schedule is safe—please try again.” Retry available; no progress lost.

Insufficient time

Revised plan includes a “Not scheduled” list with a reason per topic.

After Start Over

All data cleared; the student sees the first-use welcome and setup again.

What persists

Plan, statuses, time spent, confidence, availability and edited subjects/exams/topics stay between visits. How is a 4-spec decision.

Boundaries

One student, no accounts, nothing shared.

Acceptance test

The canonical demo scenario is the primary acceptance test. If the complete demo scenario works reliably, the POC is considered functionally successful even if non-critical edge cases or secondary UI polish remain.

What we're building vs. deferred

Building
  • Welcome + step-by-step setup with per-day availability
  • AI daily plan within daily hours
  • Dashboard: today's sessions, statuses, exam overview, completed vs. unfinished
  • Session tracking incl. actual time, confidence, partial-session remainder, Unconfirmed
  • Settings / Edit for subjects, exams, topics and availability
  • Start Over (Reset Demo)
  • Replan: Unconfirmed confirmation step, loading, affected sessions only (original → revised), explanations, Accept / Keep original, Not scheduled, on-track message, failure + Retry, rule checks on the AI's proposal
  • One canonical demo scenario
  • Plan and progress persist between visits
Deferred
  • Manual editing of the generated plan's sessions: adds edit flows and conflicts with AI replans (editing your subjects/topics is in)
  • Undo of an accepted replan: “Keep original” covers it for now
  • Attention panel and analytics: completed/unfinished is enough to prove adaptation
  • Accounts and sync: cut in scope

Later: assignments, personal goals, analytics, notifications, collaboration. Non-goals: silently assuming a skip, fake changes, cramming an unrealistic schedule.
