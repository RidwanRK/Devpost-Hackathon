The unique kernel

A study plan shouldn't break when life gets in the way. It should adapt.

Press Replan My Schedule and the AI looks at what you actually did (completed, skipped, partly done, real time spent) and rebuilds the rest of the plan. It doesn't blindly push everything to tomorrow. It protects weak, high-stakes topics, postpones what can wait, avoids unrealistic workloads, and explains the trade-offs honestly when there isn't enough time.

Who it's for, and how it flows

A university student juggling several courses and exams, like you: a Software Engineering student whose fixed timetable falls apart the first time a topic runs long.

The core adaptation loop

What the student does over and over, and why they come back: it always tells them what to do next, not what they've missed.

  1. Open the dashboard and see today's study sessions.
  2. Mark each session Complete, Skip, or Partially Done.
  3. Enter the actual time spent and update confidence in the topic.
  4. Behind? Click Replan My Schedule.
  5. Read the revised schedule: changed tasks, new time allocations, and why.

What the AI weighs

  • Upcoming exam dates
  • Topic difficulty and your confidence
  • What was completed, skipped or partly done
  • Actual time spent vs. planned
  • Study hours still available

If there isn't enough time, it says so and explains the trade-offs honestly.

Proof-of-concept boundary: Now · Later · Cut

Expand each group. To change anything, tell me which item should move and I'll update the scope.

Now In the proof of concept · 6 items
  • Scope: exam preparation and topics only.
  • Inputs: subjects, exam dates, topics, difficulty, confidence, available study hours.
  • AI daily plan built from those inputs, weak topics first.
  • Session tracking: Complete / Skip / Partially Done, plus actual time spent and confidence updates.
  • Replan My Schedule: adjusts the remaining plan from real progress and explains what changed and why.
  • Browser local storage so the plan and progress survive between visits. (Built on the MERN stack, so 4-spec decides whether MongoDB is needed for this.)
Later Worth doing, just not now · 5 items
  • Assignments and other deadlines.
  • Personal learning goals (skills outside the curriculum).
  • Advanced analytics.
  • Notifications.
  • Collaborative features.

None of these are needed to prove that the plan adapts to real progress.

Cut Explicitly left out · 1 item
  • User accounts. Unnecessary for a first version. Local storage is enough for one student's plan, and the priority is proving the plan adapts, not building a full productivity platform.

Why this matters

Balancing courses, assignments, exams and extra skills is hard, and a schedule quickly becomes unrealistic when one topic runs long or something else comes up. This is also your chosen way to practice integrating AI into a real app.

“Students need more than a fixed timetable; they need a practical way to recover when they fall behind and focus on what matters most. I want to use AI to make study planning more realistic, flexible, and less stressful.”

Stack: MERN (MongoDB, Express, React, Node), your choice, carried to 4-spec.

Look and feel: a clean dashboard that says what to do next, calm and honest in tone. Nothing more specified yet; 3-prd will fill the gaps.
